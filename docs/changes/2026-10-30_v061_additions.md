# Spec v0.4 to v0.6.1 additions

The v0.6.1 spec and its screen designs are added to the platform as new modules. Nothing in the existing nine-stage audit was replaced. This file records what was built, the decisions taken where the spec was silent or unclear, and what is still to do. It is updated as the work continues, in the same way as the 27 October change log.

Status key: **Done**, **Partly done**, **Not started**.

## What is new, by area

### Regulatory master (screens M1, M1a, M1b, M2, M4, M5)

| Screen | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| M1 Law library | Done | Every law now has version history. Changes become proposals that the Chief Auditor approves. Row-menu items "Add a source", "See clients using this law", "Set next review date" and "Assign reviewer" are not built. |
| M1a Bulk edit | Done | Tracking fields apply at once. Topics, applies-to and role levels become proposals. A reason is required. |
| M1b Add or edit a law | Done | "Draft with Claude" fills the form from official sources. It saves nothing on its own and is switched off when no Claude key is set. |
| M2 Law record | Done | Includes related court cases from the litigation tracker. |
| M4 Roles and committees | Done | The 21-role catalogue from §24.1. |
| M5 Client applicability and intro letter | Partly done | Laws in and out of scope with reasons; the intro letter is drafted, approved by the Chief Auditor and sent, and becomes item 8 of the initiation package. A second version of the letter lists what changed at the top but still carries the full letter. |

Seeded requirements, each with citation, source link and a quoted passage, cover NYC LL144, Colorado SB 26-189, Illinois HB 3773, the EU AI Act (including the 2026 amendments that move high-risk duties to 2 December 2027), GDPR, India's DPDP Act, the California CCPA automated decision-making rules and the California Civil Rights Council rules on automated-decision systems, the CERT-In Directions and NIST AI RMF.

To check: the Colorado section numbers come from the engrossed bill and are flagged until checked against the enrolled act. Smaller items are also flagged in the records: GDPR Art. 83(5) and Art. 6 mapping, the EU AI Act Art. 17 basis, two CCPA section numbers, the DPDP rule number and the Illinois rules status.

## Fixes made while integrating

- The first request after signing in was sometimes refused. Every request now saves its changes before it answers.
- A slow address lookup in new client set-up could lock the database ("database is locked"). Fixed.
- The live law refresh would have failed on its first run, because Claude's API does not return structured data and cite web sources in the same request. The refresh now searches first and structures the findings in a second step.
- The client page's contacts tab shows the client's email connection, with a link to its email groups.

## Decisions for you

1. **Go-live needs two people.** One person submits and a different Chief AI Compliance Officer approves. A company with only one CAICO and no department heads cannot take a system live.
2. **Tracing turns on at sponsor approval.** The spec says after Sales confirms the order. A "Sales confirms" step can be added.
3. **Futran Admin can manage client email groups** after onboarding too; the §26.9 table says no.
4. **Engagement Manager and Legal Reviewer land on My work**, as §13.1 gives them no home of their own.

## What IT needs to set up for real email

Not needed for test mode.

- **Futran IT, once:** register the platform in Microsoft Entra ID and in Google Cloud, then put the app IDs and secrets in the platform's settings file. Step-by-step instructions are in the README.
- **Client IT, once per client:** a Microsoft 365 administrator approves the app from the Connect button. A Google Workspace administrator marks the app as trusted, which avoids Google's security review for outside apps.

## Laptop update

- Every database change adds tables or columns only. Each was tested against a copy of the demo database with no change to existing rows.
- The role catalogue, requirements and court sources load on first start, so `run.bat` needs no extra step.
