HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT — INTERN GUIDE
PURPOSE
This document is the official Futran Solutions AI Governance Audit Template. You will complete one copy per client engagement. Every section must be filled in. If information is genuinely unavailable, write the specific reason — never leave blanks.
What You Are Doing and Why
An AI Governance Audit assesses whether a client's AI systems are built, deployed, and monitored in a responsible, safe, and legally compliant manner. Our clients — banks, hospitals, insurers, government agencies — use AI to make decisions that affect people's money, health, and rights. Your job is to gather enough documented evidence that a senior Futran consultant can form a defensible opinion about the maturity and risk level of each AI system.
Your Role as the Auditor / Intern
You do not need to be an AI engineer. You need to ask the right questions, record the answers accurately, and flag anything that seems incomplete, contradictory, or missing. When in doubt, ask the client to show you the document — not just describe it.

APPENDIX C — Glossary of Terms
PURPOSE
If you encounter a term you do not understand during the audit, look it up here first. If it is not here, write it down exactly as the client used it and flag for the Lead Auditor — never guess at a technical term's meaning.
Term
Plain English Definition
Why It Matters in AI Audit
Adversarial Attack
A deliberate input designed to trick an AI model into making an incorrect prediction
If the model is used in security, fraud detection, or medical diagnosis, adversarial robustness is critical
Algorithm
A set of rules or instructions that a computer follows to make a decision or prediction
All AI systems are built on algorithms — understanding what the algorithm does is the starting point of any audit
Agentic AI
AI that can take actions autonomously — browse the web, execute code, send emails — without human approval for each step
Much higher risk than advisory AI; human oversight controls are especially important
BAA (Business Associate Agreement)
A HIPAA-required contract between a healthcare organization and any vendor who handles patient health information on their behalf
Without a signed BAA, using a vendor for AI on PHI is a HIPAA violation
Bias (Algorithmic)
When an AI model produces systematically different (worse) outcomes for one demographic group compared to another
Can cause illegal discrimination in credit, hiring, insurance, or healthcare; must be tested before and during deployment
Data Drift
When the real-world data an AI system encounters in production starts to look different from the data it was trained on, causing performance to degrade
If untreated, the model becomes less accurate over time without anyone noticing
Data Fiduciary
Under India's DPDP Act: any entity that determines the purpose and means of processing personal data
Equivalent to a 'Data Controller' under GDPR; has specific compliance obligations
DPIA (Data Protection Impact Assessment)
A formal analysis of the privacy risks of a data processing activity, required before high-risk processing begins
Required by GDPR and evolving under India DPDP for high-risk AI systems
Disparate Impact
When a facially neutral policy or algorithm produces different outcomes for different protected groups
Measured using the 4/5ths rule: if the selection rate for the less-favored group is below 80% of the favored group, disparate impact is indicated
Explainability / XAI
The ability to describe in plain language why an AI model made a particular prediction or decision
Required by EU AI Act for high-risk AI; required by GDPR Art. 22 for automated decisions; needed for consumer adverse action notices
Feature
An input variable used by an AI model to make predictions — e.g., 'income', 'age', 'zip code', 'diagnosis code'
Some features may be proxies for protected characteristics — e.g., zip code correlates with race — creating indirect bias
Foundation Model / LLM
A large pre-trained AI model (e.g., GPT-4, Claude, Gemini) that can be adapted for many tasks
Governed under EU AI Act GPAI provisions; prompt injection and output filtering are key security controls
GenAI (Generative AI)
AI that generates new content — text, images, audio, code — rather than just classifying or predicting
Synthetic media labeling requirements apply (EU AI Act, India IT Amendment Rules 2026)
High-Risk AI (EU AI Act)
AI systems used in areas where errors can significantly harm people: healthcare, credit, employment, education, law enforcement
Subject to the strictest EU AI Act obligations — conformity assessment, documentation, human oversight, registration
HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)
US law protecting patient health information (PHI) — requires covered entities and business associates to protect PHI
Any AI touching US health data must comply; BAAs required with all vendors processing PHI
Human-in-the-Loop
A design approach where a human reviews and approves AI outputs before they are acted upon
Required by EU AI Act for high-risk systems; prevents fully automated harmful decisions
Model Card
A brief document that describes what an AI model does, how it was trained, its performance characteristics, and its limitations
Required for high-risk AI under EU AI Act; best practice for all models — ask to see it
Model Drift
When a model's predictive accuracy declines over time because the real world has changed
Requires continuous monitoring and periodic retraining; a model with no monitoring is a liability
Model Risk
The risk of adverse consequences from decisions based on poorly developed, implemented, or used AI models
Formally managed in banking (OCC SR 11-7); emerging requirement across industries
Model Validation
An independent review of an AI model to confirm it is conceptually sound, performing as intended, and suitable for its use case
Must be performed by someone not involved in building the model; required in banking, evolving in other sectors
PHI (Protected Health Information)
Under HIPAA: any individually identifiable health information — includes names, dates, diagnoses, insurance numbers, images
The most sensitive data category in US healthcare AI; any AI touching PHI requires rigorous HIPAA compliance
PII (Personally Identifiable Information)
Any data that could identify an individual — name, address, SSN, email, IP address, biometric data
Triggers privacy law obligations under GDPR, DPDP, and state laws — document carefully
Prompt Injection
An attack where malicious text in a user's input causes a GenAI system to behave in unintended ways
Critical security risk for GenAI systems; must be tested and mitigated before deployment
SHAP / LIME
Mathematical tools that explain which features most influenced a model's prediction for a specific case
Ask whether these are used — their presence indicates the organization is thinking about explainability
SDF (Significant Data Fiduciary)
Under India's DPDP Act: a Data Fiduciary classified as handling especially sensitive or large volumes of data
Subject to additional obligations including DPIAs, audits, and Data Protection Officer appointment
SOC 2 Type II
An independent audit confirming that a technology vendor has controls in place for security, availability, and privacy, and that those controls actually worked over a 6–12 month period
Required evidence from high-risk AI vendors; Type II (tested over time) is stronger than Type I (point-in-time)
Training Data
The dataset used to teach an AI model to make predictions
Its quality, bias, legal basis, and provenance are the foundation of the entire audit; get the documentation
